Filing Bankruptcy in Rutland, Vermont - Your Guide

If you live in or near Rutland, Vermont, your bankruptcy case is filed in the District of Vermont, which covers the entire state from a single courthouse in Burlington. Rutland filers rarely need to travel to Burlington - 341 meetings are conducted by Zoom, most hearings are telephonic, and the court accepts electronic filings from pro se debtors through its limited pro se filing portal. Here is what you need to know.

Vermont is one of a small group of states where filers can choose between state exemptions and the federal exemption set in 11 U.S.C. section 522(d). This is a meaningful advantage: you can compare both sets and pick whichever protects more of your specific property. For most Rutland filers with modest home equity, a paid-off car, and ordinary household goods, the federal exemptions are often the stronger choice. For filers with substantial home equity, the Vermont homestead exemption may be more generous. Run the numbers both ways before deciding.

Vermont has a single bankruptcy judge and a handful of Chapter 7 trustees. The standing Chapter 13 trustee administers all Chapter 13 cases statewide. Meetings of creditors are held by Zoom roughly 20 to 40 days after filing. The local rules are short and readable - the District of Vermont publishes them on its website - and the court is generally regarded as cooperative with both represented and pro se debtors.

Before you file, you must complete a pre-filing credit counseling course from an approved provider. A list of approved providers for the District of Vermont is available at the U.S. Trustee website. The course is roughly an hour long, usually under $25, and can be completed online from home. You will need the certificate to file.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I have to travel to Burlington to file bankruptcy in Rutland?

No. You can file electronically through an attorney, through PACER (if you have a limited pro se account), or by mailing your petition to the clerk. The 341 meeting is conducted by Zoom. Most routine hearings are telephonic. You may only need to appear in person for a contested matter, and even those are rare in consumer cases.

Can I use the federal exemptions in a Vermont bankruptcy?

Yes. Vermont is one of the states that has not opted out of the federal exemption scheme, meaning filers can choose between the Vermont state exemptions in 12 V.S.A. and the federal exemptions in 11 U.S.C. section 522(d). You pick one set - you cannot mix - but the choice itself is a meaningful advantage.

How much does bankruptcy cost in Rutland?

The court filing fee is $338 for Chapter 7 and $313 for Chapter 13. Attorney fees vary. Typical Chapter 7 attorney fees in Vermont run $1,000 to $2,000 for a straightforward consumer case. Chapter 13 attorney fees are usually higher because of the plan confirmation process and five-year trustee relationship - $3,000 to $4,500 is common, often paid partially through the plan itself.
